Most listing shoots don't go wrong because of the camera. They go wrong because of a toaster, a pile of mail, and three different bulb colors in one room. This real estate photo shoot checklist for NJ homes is the list we'd hand every seller and agent the day before we show up in Jersey City, Hoboken, Weehawken or anywhere else in Hudson County.

It covers photos and video. Video is less forgiving. A still can hide a messy corner with a crop. A slow walk-through shows everything.

## Why prep matters more than gear

A wide lens sees the whole room. That's the point. It also sees the dish rack, the charging cables and the shoes by the door.

When a home is prepped, the shoot is faster and the edit is cleaner. We spend time on angles and light instead of moving laundry baskets between rooms. And buyers scrolling Zillow or Instagram see the space, not the stuff in it.

A good rule: if it wouldn't be there in a hotel room, it shouldn't be there on shoot day.

## The real estate photo shoot checklist NJ sellers can use the night before

Do this the evening before. Shoot mornings always feel shorter than you think.

**Whole house**
- Replace dead bulbs. Use the same color temperature in each room, so you don't get one warm lamp next to a cold ceiling light.
- Open every blind and curtain to the same height.
- Clean windows and glass doors, inside at least.
- Hide cords, chargers, remotes, trash cans and pet bowls.
- Turn off ceiling fans and TVs. Spinning fans blur in photos and look busy on video.
- Take down personal photos, kids' art on the fridge, and anything with names on it.

**Outside**
- Move cars out of the driveway and off the curb in front of the house if you can.
- Put away hoses, bins, toys and tools.
- Sweep the front steps and walkway. Turn on porch lights.
- In fall, rake the leaves the day before, not the week before.

## Kitchen and bathrooms: where most shots are won or lost

Kitchens sell homes, and they are the room people zoom into. Treat it like a showroom.

![Clean white kitchen counters ready for a real estate shoot in New Jersey](media/blog/nj-kitchen-styled-counter.jpg)

**Kitchen**
- Clear the counters almost completely. One or two simple things are fine: a plant, a cutting board, a bowl of fruit.
- Put away the dish rack, sponges, soap bottles, paper towels and small appliances.
- Clean the stainless steel. Fingerprints show up on fridge and dishwasher doors, especially on video.
- Turn on under-cabinet lights if you have them. They add depth to the backsplash.
- Empty the sink.

**Bathrooms**
- Close every toilet lid.
- Remove shampoo bottles, razors, toothbrushes and bath mats.
- Hang one set of clean, matching towels. White or neutral works best.
- Wipe mirrors and glass shower doors. Streaks catch the light.

## Bedrooms, living room and small spaces

- Make beds tight, with simple bedding. Too many pillows look cluttered on a wide lens.
- Clear nightstands down to a lamp and maybe one book.
- Remove anything stored under beds that shows.
- In the living room, line up furniture with the walls and straighten rugs.
- Clear coffee tables down to one or two items.
- Closets: if they're part of the listing story (walk-in, built-ins), clean them out enough to show space. If not, close the doors.

Small apartments in Jersey City and Manhattan need this even more. In 600 square feet, every extra object takes up visible space. Less stuff reads as more room.

## Light, time of day and season

Daylight does most of the work in a listing shoot. A few things help:

- Book the shoot when the main rooms get soft light. In many NJ and NYC apartments, that's mid-morning or early afternoon. Your photographer can check the orientation on a map before picking a time.
- Turn on all interior lights during the shoot, even in daytime. It warms up the rooms and fills dark corners.
- In winter, light goes fast. Aim for an earlier start so the exterior shots don't end up dark and flat.
- If the home has a great view or a lit-up exterior, ask about a short twilight session.

## What changes when you add video

Video needs everything above, plus a little more:

- **Quiet.** If the listing video includes a voiceover or an agent intro, turn off AC units, dishwashers and music. Tell neighbors if you can.
- **Clear paths.** We often move slowly through the home with a stabilizer. Hallways and doorways need to be open, with nothing on the floor.
- **No people or pets in the frame.** Plan for kids and pets to be out of the house during the walk-through.
- **Think about formats.** One shoot can cover a horizontal tour for Zillow and YouTube and vertical clips for Instagram Reels and TikTok. Tell your crew upfront which ones you need, so they frame for both.
- **Agent on camera?** If the realtor is doing an intro, pick one or two spots in the home with good light and a clean background.

## The shoot-day morning: 15-minute final sweep

Right before the crew arrives:

1. Walk every room in the order a buyer would.
2. Open blinds, turn on lights, turn off fans and TVs.
3. Check counters, sinks and toilet lids one more time.
4. Put the last-minute stuff (keys, coffee cups, phones, bags) in one box and put that box in a car or a closet that won't be filmed.
5. Move cars out front.

Then step out of the way and let the crew work room by room.

## FAQ

**How long before the shoot should I start preparing my home?**
Start decluttering a few days before, and do the final checklist the night before. Leave 15 minutes on shoot morning for a last sweep.

**Should I be home during the real estate photo shoot?**
You can be, but it helps to stay out of the rooms being shot, especially during video. Pets and kids should be out of the house during the walk-through.

**Do I need to stage my home for photos and video?**
Not always. A clean, clutter-free home with good light already looks much better on camera. Staging helps most with empty homes or rooms with an unclear use.
