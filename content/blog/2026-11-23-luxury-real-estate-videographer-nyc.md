A broker sends the link for a Tribeca loft. The video opens on the lobby door. Then a hallway. Then a coat closet. By the time the living room shows up, the buyer's assistant has already closed the tab.

If you're hiring a luxury real estate videographer in NYC, this is the problem you're paying to solve. **At $5M and up, the video isn't a tour. It's the first showing.** Here's what agents in Manhattan and Hudson County should expect, and what to ask before anyone sets up a tripod.

## 1. The first three seconds decide everything

Most listing videos start where the walkthrough starts: the front door. That's the order you'd show it in person. On a phone, it's the slowest possible open.

Open on the single best thing the property has. The view from the corner window. The double-height living room. The terrace at dusk.

- Pick the "hero" shot before the shoot, not in the edit.
- Put it first, even if it's the last room on the floor plan.
- Save the entry, the hallway and the closets for the long version, if at all.

Redfin's writers quote a videographer who puts it simply: buyers decide in the first few seconds, so they never open on the front door. We agree.

## 2. A luxury real estate videographer in NYC sells the light, not the square footage

A $5M buyer has seen a hundred white kitchens. What they haven't seen is how *this* apartment feels at 4 p.m. in November.

**That means scheduling the shoot around the sun, not around the photographer's calendar.** In Manhattan, a south-facing unit and a north-facing unit on the same floor need different shoot times. A Hudson County high-rise looking at the skyline needs the hour when the city lights come on and there's still color in the sky.

What to ask your videographer:

- Which rooms do you shoot first, and why?
- Will you come back for a twilight pass, or do it the same day?
- How do you handle mixed light (warm lamps, cool window light)?

If they don't have a clear answer, you'll get flat footage. We wrote more on timing in [Best Time to Photograph a House](/blog/best-time-to-photograph-a-house).

## 3. Details are the luxury, so film them like product

Most agents get this wrong: they spend the whole shoot on wide shots. Wides show the layout. They don't show the money.

At this price, the money is in the details. Hardware. Stone. Millwork. The way a door closes. Film those like a jewelry ad: slow, close, with real light on the texture.

A good split for a high-end listing: roughly half wides and room flows, half details and moments. If your reel is 90% wides, it will look like every other listing on StreetEasy.

## 4. Show the life, but keep it believable

Lifestyle footage is where luxury listing videos go wrong in both directions. Some are empty rooms with music. Others are a full fashion shoot where you can't see the apartment.

Aim for the middle. One or two quiet moments: a hand pouring wine on the terrace, someone reading by the window, the kitchen with a coffee going. Real actions, real speed. **The apartment stays the main character.**

## 5. Drone shots in NYC: plan them, don't promise them

Clients love aerials. In New York City they're also the hardest thing to deliver. Much of the city sits in controlled airspace near the major airports, and the city has its own rules on where drones can take off and land. Hudson County waterfront has its own airspace issues too.

What to do:

- Ask your videographer to check the exact address before the shoot, not on the day. The FAA's [B4UFLY tool](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/b4ufly) is the starting point.
- Don't put "drone footage" in your listing presentation until it's confirmed.
- Have a plan B: a high floor, a terrace, a rooftop across the street. Often the view from inside is stronger than an aerial anyway.

## 6. Deliver more than one video

One three-minute film on YouTube isn't the deliverable anymore. Ask for a set:

- **The main film** (horizontal): 1.5 to 3 minutes, for the listing page, email and YouTube.
- **A vertical cut** (9:16): 20 to 45 seconds for Instagram Reels and TikTok, hero shot first.
- **Two or three short teasers**: one room or one detail each, for stories and paid ads.
- **Stills pulled from the footage** for social posts, on top of the full photo set.

Same shoot day, four or five uses. That's how a single listing keeps showing up for two weeks instead of two days.

## 7. Prep the property like a hotel, not a home

The best luxury listings look like boutique hotel suites: calm, clean, every surface intentional. We shoot hotels too, and the prep is the same.

### Quick checklist: the day before the shoot

- Clear every counter down to 2-3 objects
- Replace any dead or mismatched bulbs (same color temperature everywhere)
- Fresh flowers in one or two places, not everywhere
- Hide cords, remotes, toiletries, kids' and pet items
- Windows cleaned inside and out where possible
- Building access, doorman and elevator booked for the crew

## FAQ

**How long should a luxury listing video be?**
For the main film, 1.5 to 3 minutes is enough for most apartments and townhouses. For Instagram and TikTok, cut a 20 to 45 second vertical version that opens on the best shot.

**Can you fly a drone over a Manhattan listing?**
Sometimes, but often not. Airspace and city takeoff rules depend on the exact location. A videographer should check the address before the shoot and have a non-drone plan for the view.

**Should a luxury listing video have people in it?**
A few quiet, natural moments help buyers picture living there. Keep them short and keep the space as the focus, not the model.
