A seller in a Jersey City condo asks you: "Are you doing a video for my place?" You know the other agent they interviewed promised one. You also know the last listing video you paid for got 40 views.

So, do you need **real estate video in Jersey City**, or are good photos enough? The honest answer: photos do most of the selling. Video does a different job. If you know which job you're hiring it for, you'll stop wasting money on it.

This guide is for agents and brokers in Jersey City, Hoboken, Weehawken and the rest of Hudson County, plus anyone listing across the river in Manhattan or Brooklyn.

## First, ignore the "403% more inquiries" stat

Search this topic and you'll see the same numbers on every page: listings with video get "403% more inquiries" or sell "31% faster."

We tried to find where they come from. They're repeated by companies that sell video tools, usually with no study, no date and no market attached. That doesn't mean video doesn't help. It means **nobody has shown that video alone sells a home faster.**

What agents on forums like r/realtors keep saying is more useful than any vendor stat:

- Bad pricing beats good video every time. A video won't fix a listing priced well over the comps.
- Bad photos actively hurt, especially in a market where buyers scroll past hundreds of polished listings.
- Video helps most when it gets the listing seen: social media, email, and buyers who can't come by easily.

## What photos do that video can't

Buyers start on Zillow, Realtor.com or StreetEasy, and they start with photos. They swipe fast, compare, and decide which homes make the shortlist. A video can't do that job, because nobody watches 20 videos to build a shortlist.

Photos win at:

- **The first click.** The cover photo is your listing's headline.
- **Comparing.** Buyers flip between three kitchens side by side. You can't do that with a video.
- **Detail.** A still lets people zoom into the counter, the floor, the hardware.

## What real estate video does that photos can't

Photos show rooms. Video shows how the rooms connect. That matters more in Jersey City and Hoboken than in most places, because so many listings are condos and brownstone floors where layout is the whole question:

- How far is the bedroom from the living room?
- Is the "open kitchen" really open, or a corridor?
- What do you actually see out the window: the Manhattan skyline, or the building next door?

That last one is big. A photo of a window view can look edited. A slow, steady shot walking up to the window, with the city moving outside, is hard to fake.

![Hotel suite bedroom with a large window and a green view](media/blog/bedroom-window-view-photo.jpg)
*A photo can show the bed and the window. Only video shows walking in and seeing the view open up.*

Video also travels where photos don't. Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts push video to people who weren't searching for a home today. That's how you reach the buyer who lives in Manhattan and hasn't started looking in Jersey City yet.

## When real estate video in Jersey City is worth it

Not every listing needs a video. Here's how we'd decide.

**Video is worth it when:**

- The home has a view, a terrace, a roof deck or outdoor space
- The layout is unusual (duplex, split floor plan, brownstone with stairs)
- Buyers are likely to be out of area: relocating from Manhattan, Brooklyn or out of state
- It's a new development or a price point where sellers expect a full marketing package
- You want the listing to work as content for *your* brand, not just this sale

**Photos are probably enough when:**

- It's a standard rental or a quick-moving starter unit
- The space is small and photos show all of it
- The home isn't ready (mid-renovation, cluttered, tenants still in it)

Most agents get this wrong in one direction: they skip video on the listings that need it most, and spend on it for listings where it adds nothing.

## How long should the video be?

Short. Most listing videos are too long because they show every room at the same speed.

- **Instagram Reels / TikTok:** 15 to 30 seconds, vertical. Open on the best thing (the view, the kitchen), not the front door.
- **MLS, Zillow, website:** 60 to 90 seconds. Enough to understand the layout.
- **YouTube or luxury listings:** 2 minutes at most, unless you're on camera as the agent.

**Common mistake:** starting the video outside the building with a slow drone shot. On a phone, people scroll away before they get to the apartment. Lead with the room that sells it.

## Quick checklist: shoot day for photos + video

- Book one visit for both. Same day, same staging, same light.
- Shoot when the main rooms get soft daylight, not hard direct sun.
- Turn on every light, including under-cabinet and closet lights.
- Open blinds fully if the view is a selling point. Close them if it isn't.
- Clear counters, hide cords, remove personal photos.
- Plan the video path before filming: front door, main living space, kitchen, best bedroom, view.
- Ask the seller to be out for the hour. Fewer people in reflections.
- Get vertical clips for social at the same time as the horizontal tour.

## FAQ

**Do listings with video sell faster in Jersey City?**
There's no solid local study proving it. The widely shared stats come from video vendors. Video helps a listing get seen and shows layout and views, but price and photos still drive most decisions.

**Should I get real estate photos or video first?**
Photos. Every listing needs strong photos for Zillow, StreetEasy and the MLS. Add video when the home has a view, outdoor space, an unusual layout, or out-of-area buyers.

**How long should a real estate listing video be?**
15 to 30 seconds for Instagram and TikTok, 60 to 90 seconds for your website and listing portals, and around two minutes at most for luxury listings.
