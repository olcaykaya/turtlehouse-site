The kitchen is done. The homeowner is walking around touching the new counters, telling you it's better than the rendering. You pull out your phone, ask them to "say a few words," and they go stiff: "Yeah, they did a great job. Very professional."

That clip will never win you a job. A good **kitchen remodel testimonial video** is a short, honest story from a homeowner that answers the questions your next client is too polite to ask. This guide is for remodelers, kitchen and bath showrooms and builders in New Jersey, from Hoboken and Jersey City to Bergen County and Middlesex County, who want videos that sound like real people, not ads.

## Why a kitchen remodel testimonial video works better than reviews

Written reviews are everywhere, and buyers know some are fake or cherry-picked. Video is harder to fake. People see a real face, a real house and a real kitchen.

But the bigger reason is fear. A homeowner pricing a full kitchen remodel isn't worried that you'll get four stars instead of five. They're worried about:

- Being overcharged or hit with surprise costs
- Crews not showing up, or the job dragging on for months
- Dust and damage in the rest of the house
- Living without a kitchen for weeks

**The best testimonial answers one of those fears, in the homeowner's own words.** A line like "We were scared about living without a kitchen with two kids. They set up a temporary sink in the dining room" does more than any compliment.

## Mistake 1–3: asking at the wrong time, the wrong way

**1. Asking on the last day.** On punch-list day there's still tape on the floor and the homeowner is tired. Wait one to three weeks. They've cooked in the kitchen, the dust is gone, and they still remember how stressful it was.

**2. Asking for "a quick video."** That sounds like work. Ask for a short conversation about how the project went, and say it takes about 15 minutes of their time. Tell them they can see it before anything is posted.

**3. Forgetting the paperwork.** Get a simple signed release before you film. It should say where the video can be used (website, social media, ads), whether you can show their face, first name and town, and that you won't show the house number or street. Many homeowners say yes once they know their address stays private.

## Mistake 4–5: questions that get bland answers

**4. Yes/no questions.** "Were you happy with the work?" gets "Yes." Ask questions that start a story instead.

Use this order. It follows the way people actually tell the story:

1. What was wrong with the old kitchen? What made you finally do it?
2. What worried you most before you hired anyone?
3. Why did you pick this company over the others?
4. What was the hardest week, and how was it handled?
5. What's one thing you do in this kitchen now that you couldn't before?
6. What would you tell a neighbor who's thinking about a remodel?

**5. Feeding them lines.** Never hand over a script. Instead, ask them to answer in full sentences ("The thing that worried us most was...") so the answer makes sense without your question. Then ask the same thing twice in different words. The second answer is usually the real one.

Mistakes and problems are fine. A homeowner saying "the tile was back-ordered for two weeks, but they called us before we had to ask" is gold. It shows how you handle trouble, which is what people are really buying.

## Mistake 6: bad light and bad sound

Most testimonial videos fail on sound, not picture. People forgive a slightly soft image. They click away from echo and hum.

![Matte black faucet and quartz counter in a finished New Jersey kitchen remodel](media/blog/kitchen-remodel-faucet-detail.jpg)
*Close details like hardware and counters are the B-roll that makes the interview feel real.*

**Sound**
- Use a small lav mic clipped to the homeowner's shirt. Not the camera mic.
- Turn off the fridge hum if you can, plus the dishwasher, the range hood and any music.
- New kitchens echo: hard counters, tile, no curtains. Film in the kitchen, but put the person near a rug or a sofa in the next room if the echo is bad.

**Light**
- Sit the homeowner facing a window, or at an angle to it. Never with the window behind them.
- Turn on the under-cabinet lights. They make the backsplash glow in the background.
- Pick a time when no direct sun hits the face. Soft, indirect window light looks best.

## Mistake 7: no B-roll, no before footage

A talking head for two minutes is hard to watch. What keeps people watching is cutting away to the kitchen while the homeowner talks.

**Film the "before" while you still can.** This is the step most remodelers skip. On demo day, shoot slow, steady clips of the old kitchen from the same spots you'll shoot the finished one. Same height, same angle. Later, those matching shots make the before/after cut that people stop scrolling for.

![Herringbone floor and stainless range in a remodeled kitchen](media/blog/kitchen-remodel-herringbone-floor.jpg)
*Floors, seams and finishes are what other homeowners zoom in on. Give them a slow, close look.*

### Quick checklist: B-roll shot list for a finished kitchen

- One wide shot from the doorway (the "walk-in" view)
- Slow push toward the island or the range
- Drawers and cabinets opening and closing (soft-close sells)
- Close-ups: faucet, hardware, counter edge, backsplash seams, floor pattern
- Under-cabinet lights switching on
- The homeowner actually using the kitchen: making coffee, cooking, kids at the counter
- Matching angles of the "before" from demo day

## Mistake 8–9: one long video, posted once

**8. Only making the long version.** A 60–120 second edit is right for your website and for estimate follow-up emails. Prospects comparing three bids will watch it. But on Instagram and TikTok, nobody watches two minutes of a stranger.

From one shoot, cut:

- **Website / YouTube:** 60–120 seconds, horizontal (16:9)
- **Instagram Reels / TikTok:** 15–30 seconds, vertical (9:16), the single best answer up front, with captions on screen
- **Feed carousel:** before/after photos plus one quote card from the interview (only words the homeowner actually said)
- **Google Business Profile:** a short clip and a few finished photos

Frame the interview wide enough so you can crop a vertical version later, with the face in the middle third.

**9. Posting once and forgetting it.** Put the video on the service page that matches the job. A kitchen testimonial belongs on your kitchen page, not buried on a "testimonials" page. Send it with your estimates. Pin it on Instagram.

## FAQ

**How long should a kitchen remodel testimonial video be?**
About 60–120 seconds for your website and sales emails. For Instagram Reels or TikTok, cut a 15–30 second vertical version that opens with the strongest answer.

**When should I ask a homeowner for a testimonial video?**
One to three weeks after the project is finished. The kitchen is clean, they've used it, and they still remember the process clearly.

**What if the homeowner doesn't want to be on camera?**
Offer options: film only their hands and the kitchen while they talk, use audio over B-roll, or show just their first name and town. Many people who say no to a face on camera will say yes to that.
